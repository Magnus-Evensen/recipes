## Canonical definition

Meat braised in a sauce of rehydrated, blended dried chiles, seasoned with cumin and oregano.
That's the whole dish. It took shape in 19th-century San Antonio, where the "Chili Queens" sold
it from plaza stands, and the Texas purist version is meat, dried chiles and spices only: no beans,
no tomatoes.

Beef is the Texan default, but pork is not a stranger to the family. Two established dishes are
essentially the same construction with pork:

- **Puerco en chile colorado** (northern Mexico, also Texas Mexican) — cubed pork shoulder in a
  guajillo-led red chile sauce. *Colorado* means "red-coloured" and refers to the sauce.
- **Carne adovada** (New Mexico) — pork shoulder marinated in New Mexico red chile, then
  slow-braised.

So a pork chili con carne is closer to chile colorado than to a substitution. The neighbouring
dish to keep apart is the European/Norwegian weeknight chili: minced meat, canned tomatoes,
kidney beans and chili powder. That's a different dish that borrowed the name.

**Source weakness:** origin-language (Spanish) sources were thin. Mexican sources cover guajillo
and chile colorado but say little about *chile con carne*, which is a Texan dish and mostly
documented in English. Treat the matrix as solid on technique and softer on regional detail.

## Variation matrix

| Axis | Consensus | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meat | Beef chuck, cubed | Chuck · coarse-ground chuck · beef + pork · pork shoulder | Pork shoulder is standard in chile colorado and adovada; mixed beef-pork appears in Saveur and Food Network |
| Cut size | 1–2.5 cm cubes | Coarse grind → 4 cm cubes | Purists reject fine mince |
| Chile base | Dried chiles, soaked and blended | Ancho · guajillo · pasilla · New Mexico · árbol for heat | Ancho for sweetness and body, guajillo for colour and tang; chili powder is the shortcut |
| Soaking liquid | Water or stock | Water · chicken stock · beef stock | Beef stock is common in Texan recipes — a hidden-beef trap |
| Spices | Cumin + Mexican oregano | Some add coriander, cinnamon, smoked paprika | Cumin is the Texas signature; chile colorado uses less |
| Tomato | None (Texas purist) | None · tomato paste · roasted tomato · canned | Medrano's pork chile colorado blends roasted tomato into the sauce; European chili is tomato-led |
| Beans | None (Texas purist) | None · on the side · in the pot | The San Antonio stands served chile con carne with beans as an option |
| Thickener | Masa harina | Masa · flour · reduction alone | Breaking up some meat does the same job |
| Acid / sweetness | Little in beef chili | Vinegar · orange · raisins · brown sugar | Pork versions lean on acid and fruit to make up for milder meat |
| Cook time | 2–3 h | 1.5 h (shoulder) – 4 h | Pork shoulder tenderises faster than chuck, unless it's lean |

## Base recipe

The synthesis before the first cook: chile colorado's structure with Texas seasoning. Serves 4,
about two dinners for two.

**Meat**
- 1.1 kg pork shoulder (*svinenakke* preferred), 2 cm cubes, fat left on
- 8 g salt to pre-salt (≈0.7% of meat weight)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil or lard
- 100 g bacon, diced (optional)

**Chile base**
- 4 dried ancho, 4 dried guajillo, 2 dried pasilla (≈85 g total)
- 1 chile de árbol (optional, the only real heat)
- 750 ml low-salt chicken stock (500 ml to soak, 250 ml to braise)

**Seasoning**
- 1 large onion, 5 garlic cloves
- 1 tbsp cumin seeds, toasted and ground; 1 tsp Mexican oregano; 1 bay leaf
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tbsp masa harina in 60 ml water
- 1 tbsp cider vinegar, salt to finish

**Method:** pre-salt the meat. Stem, seed and toast the chiles, soak in hot stock, blend smooth.
Brown the pork hard in batches. Soften the onion, bloom garlic and spices, fry the purée until it
darkens. Braise with the remaining stock, bay and fish sauce at a bare simmer until a cube breaks
under a spoon (1.5–2.5 h). Thicken with masa, finish with vinegar and salt.

**Contested calls:**
- **Pork, not beef** — a dietary constraint, but well precedented (see above).
- **Shoulder only** — the collagen and fat do what chuck's do. Loin dries out.
- **Chicken stock, never beef** — beef stock and bouillon are the hidden-beef risks in Texan
  recipes.
- **Fish sauce and vinegar** — pork is sweeter and milder than beef; the pork chile dishes all add
  acid or fruit. Least traditional choice in the file.
- **Salt** — stock, fish sauce and bacon all add salt, so pre-salt light and season at the end,
  after the acid.

## My deviations

**First cook, October 2026.** Details beyond these weren't recorded; otherwise the base recipe
was followed as planned.

- **Tomatoes in.** The 250 ml braising stock was replaced with a 400 g can of crushed tomatoes,
  vinegar cut to an optional teaspoon. Aim was a familiar, European-leaning first version.
  Costs some chile clarity; has precedent in pork chile colorado.
- **Chiles: 4 ancho, 1 guajillo, no pasilla.** Made up with 1½–2 tbsp sweet Hungarian paprika
  fried with the purée, up to ½ tsp smoked paprika, and ~10 g 70% dark chocolate stirred in at
  the end for the pasilla's bitter depth. **Taste was great** — the substitution held.
- **No masa.** Pressing a third to half of the cubes against the pot thickened it enough.
- **Black beans, in the pot.** No pinto available, kidney not liked. Fit well; keep as the
  alternative to pinto.
- **Undercooked.** Meat was firm, not tender. The shoulder was lean for shoulder and needed
  longer than the 1.5–2.5 h window. Pick fattier *svinenakke*, and go by the spoon test, not the
  clock.
- **Yield:** about 4 servings, matching the plan.
- **Served** with tortilla chips, guacamole and diced raw onion. The second eater wanted rice
  with it and no guacamole: with chips only it read as meat-heavy, against the expectation of a
  European-style chili.

## Open questions

1. **Cook time with a properly fatty shoulder.** Does it land inside 1.5–2.5 h, or is ~3 h the
   honest target?
2. **Full chile set.** Next time with 4 guajillo and 2 pasilla — is it noticeably better than the
   paprika-and-chocolate version, or was the substitute just as good?
3. **Bacon and árbol** — not recorded whether used. Test one with, one without.
4. **Tomato dose.** One can kept it familiar. Would 2 tbsp tomato paste instead move it closer to
   chile colorado without losing the household?
5. **Bean amount** — one can or one and a half with black beans.
