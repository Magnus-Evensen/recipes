> **Source quality: moderate-to-weak.** Five recipes plus one chain-restaurant description. The
> Japanese-language ones are mass-market recipe sites and municipal handouts, not yakitori
> specialists. I found no chef-level source for the *don* specifically. The matrix is reliable for
> "what Japanese home cooks do", weak for "what a yakitori-ya does".

## Canonical definition

Grilled chicken thigh and *naganegi* (長ねぎ, Japanese long onion) in *tare* (たれ, the sweet
soy glaze of yakitori), off the skewer, over plain short-grain rice, usually with shredded nori.
There is no fixed blueprint: one source describes it as a recent izakaya dish that each shop
builds its own way.

The neighbour it must be distinguished from is **teriyaki chicken don**. The sauces are nearly
the same ingredients. What separates them is method: yakitori is dry-heat grilled and the tare is
applied to already-browned meat, where it scorches slightly; teriyaki is glazed by reducing the
sauce around the meat in a pan. Most home recipes for yakitori don use the pan method, so in a
home kitchen the negi and a hard sear are what carry the identity.

## Base recipe

Serves 2 for dinner in this household, 3 standard portions (see the portion note below). About
15 min prep, 15 min cooking. Pan version with
skinless thigh, chosen by Magnus.

**Tare**
- 45 ml Japanese soy sauce (koikuchi, e.g. Kikkoman)
- 45 ml mirin
- 45 ml ryorishu (salted cooking sake)
- 12 g sugar
- 10 g ginger, finely grated (about 2 tsp)

**Chicken**
- 400 g boneless, skinless chicken thigh, in 3 cm pieces
- 0.8 g fine salt (0.2%, halved because the ryorishu is salted)
- 5 g cornflour or potato starch (about ½ tbsp)
- 2 thin leeks (about 250 g trimmed), white and pale green, in 2.5 cm rounds
- 1 tbsp neutral oil

**Bowl**
- 270 g Japanese short-grain rice (raw weight), cooked plain
- Kizami nori, or a sheet of nori cut into thin strips
- Shichimi togarashi
- A little of the leek's inner pale green, sliced very fine and rinsed
- 3 egg yolks (optional)

1. Stir the tare together until the sugar dissolves.
2. Salt the chicken and leave it 15 minutes while the rice cooks. Pat dry, then toss with the
   starch just before cooking.
3. Large stainless pan, medium-high heat, half the oil in once the pan is hot. Leek rounds
   cut-side down until browned, turn, brown the other face.
4. Add 50 ml water, lid on, and steam until a knife tip meets no resistance, 4–5 minutes. Add a
   splash more water if the pan dries first. Lift the leek out and wipe the pan.
5. Rest of the oil in. Chicken in one layer, left alone until well browned and released, then
   flipped and cooked until just about done through.
6. Tare in, leek back, and reduce for 1–2 minutes, turning everything through the sauce, until
   it is a thin syrup that still pools when the pan is tilted. Take it off the heat there; with
   starch it tightens fast and keeps thickening as it sits. Do not add water at this stage.
7. Rice in the bowl, nori, then chicken and leek, and the loose sauce spooned over so some
   reaches the rice.
   Shichimi, sliced leek, yolk if using.

**Portion note:** the sources use 125–150 g chicken per person, so 400 g is three standard
portions. Test 1 confirmed it as one dinner for two here, with Magnus eating 1.5–2 portions and
nothing left over. For dinner plus a lunch, scale everything to 600 g chicken (multiply by 1.5).

**Reasoning for the contested calls**

- **Pan, tare reduced around the chicken.** The home consensus (4/5) and Magnus's call. The cost
  is that the result sits closer to teriyaki. A hard sear before any liquid goes in is what is
  left to separate them.
- **Large stainless pan, one layer.** Chicken plus leek is 650 g, too much for the small carbon
  steel pan without steaming, and a soy reduction is hard on carbon steel seasoning. Preheat
  properly before the oil; starch-dusted chicken sticks to a cool stainless pan.
- **Starch dusting.** 3/5 sources do it. With skin-on thigh I would leave it out because it dulls
  the skin. Skinless has no skin to protect and no rendered fat, so the starch earns its place:
  it gives the surface something to brown and makes the glaze cling.
- **A full tablespoon of oil.** Skinless thigh renders very little, and the leek has nothing to
  brown in otherwise.
- **Leek steamed separately, before the chicken.** No source does this, because naganegi cooks
  in the 2–3 minutes the sauce takes. Leek does not: in test 1 a 2-minute lid step in the tare
  left it underdone, and cooking on to fix it over-reduced the sauce. Steaming it first in plain
  water takes the leek out of the sauce timing altogether.
- **Short reduction.** A consequence of the above. The tare is on the heat for 1–2 minutes only,
  which keeps the starch from turning gluey and leaves the ginger some aroma.
- **45 ml soy and mirin, 12 g sugar.** Soy : mirin is unanimously 1 : 1. Volume was raised by half
  after test 1 and now sits at the top of the source range (2¼ tbsp soy per 300 g), because the
  sources have less leek to coat and the bowl needs some sauce for the rice. Sugar is unchanged
  in proportion, about 1¼ tsp per tbsp soy.
- **Ryorishu for the sake.** What Magnus had. It is sake with roughly 2–3% salt added, so 45 ml
  brings about 1 g of salt; the pre-salt is halved to 0.8 g to compensate. Some brands
  also add sweetener, which would push the tare sweeter. With drinking sake, go back to 1.6 g
  (0.4%). If there is neither, two of five sources leave sake out; use water for the volume.
- **Ginger in the tare.** Magnus's call, backed by 2/5 sources (one in the tare, one in a
  marinade). Amount follows the source that puts it in the tare: about 1 tsp per 300 g chicken.
  In test 1 it was barely noticeable at 8 g, but the sauce was cooked far longer than intended.
  Raised to 10 g with the larger tare; judge it again after a short reduction. No garlic.
- **Salt budget.** 45 ml koikuchi carries roughly 7.5 g salt, now shared between chicken, leek
  and the top of the rice. Saltiness at the 30 ml level was not reported as a problem in test 1;
  check it again at this volume and drop the pre-salt first if it is too much.

**Sourcing notes**

- **Soy:** the Lee Kum Kee light soy in the cupboard is a different product (less wheat, sharper).
  For a sauce that is one-third soy this matters. Buy a koikuchi.
- **Mirin:** check the label. *Hon mirin* is about 14% alcohol and its sweetness is from rice.
  "Mirin-style seasoning" is mostly glucose syrup and noticeably sweeter; if that is what the shop
  has, drop the sugar to 8 g.
- **Sake:** any cheap drinking sake. Cooking sake (*ryorishu*) is salted, so cut the pre-salt to
  0.2% if using it.

**Storage:** cooked chicken and leek are fine the next day for lunch. One source rates it a poor
freezer dish.

## My deviations

- **Skinless thigh** (what was in the fridge). Every source uses or implies skin-on, and the
  crisp skin and its fat are a good part of what reads as yakitori. Skinless gives a leaner,
  softer result that is closer to teriyaki chicken. Compensated with starch and more oil. For a
  skin-on run: drop the starch, start skin-side down in a film of oil, and pour off the rendered
  fat before the tare goes in.
- **Leek for naganegi.** Leek is the usual stand-in outside Japan. It is thicker, more fibrous
  and less sweet, with tighter layers, so it needs longer than the 2–3 minutes the sources give
  naganegi. Hence thin leeks, 2.5 cm rounds and the lid step. Dark green tops are too tough here;
  keep them for stock. The raw garnish is also sharper than scallion, so slice it fine and rinse.

## Test log

- **Test 1, 2026-10-04/05.** 400 g skinless thigh, leek, 30 ml tare with ryorishu and ginger.
  Enjoyed; chicken good, salt and sweetness right. Too little sauce and it went gluey. Cause:
  the leek was not cooked through after the lid step, so the sauce was cooked on with added
  water and over-reduced. Ginger barely noticeable. Fed two for dinner, no leftovers.
  Changes: leek browned and steamed on its own first; tare up by half; reduction cut to 1–2
  minutes and stopped while loose; ginger 8 g to 10 g.
- **Test 2, 2026-10-19.** Test 1 changes as written: leek steamed first, 45 ml tare, 1–2 minute
  reduction. Better with this amount of sauce. Graduated 2026-11-09, with the nori moved to go
  in last, on top of the yolk.

## Open questions

1. **Skin-on against skinless.** The biggest untested variable. With skin, the question becomes
   whether it survives the reduction; if not, pull the chicken while the tare reduces.
2. **Starch.** Went gluey in test 1, but under a long over-reduction. If it is still gluey after
   a 1–2 minute reduction, halve it or drop it.
3. **Sugar level.** Right in test 1 at 8 g per 30 ml soy; kept in proportion (12 g per 45 ml).
4. **Ginger.** Run it once without to see what the plain tare tastes like.
5. **Broiler version.** Dry-broiled chicken and leek, tare reduced separately and brushed on for
   the last two minutes. The route to a result that is clearly not teriyaki; untested.
6. **Leek texture.** If the rounds fall apart or stay stringy, halve them lengthwise into planks.
7. **Sansho instead of shichimi,** and whether the yolk earns its place or just mutes the tare.
8. **Tsukune.** The chain version is thigh, tsukune and skin together. A weekend version.
