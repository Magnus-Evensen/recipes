## Canonical definition

A dense, chocolate-led bar baked in a pan, sitting between cake and confection. The texture
spectrum is the whole argument: **fudgy** comes from a high ratio of fat and chocolate to flour,
**cakey** from more flour plus leavening, **chewy** from sugar and well-beaten eggs. The brief
here is fudgy + chewy + gooey, a shiny crackly top, bittersweet with coffee, chocolate chunks, no
nuts, gluten-free, baked the day before and reheated. The gooey comes from fat and chocolate that
is soft when warm, not from raw batter. See the contested calls below.

Gluten is not load-bearing in a fudgy brownie. Flour is a small share of the batter and the
structure comes mostly from eggs and cocoa solids. One source goes further and calls this a recipe
where working without gluten is an advantage for chew (ckbk). The hard part is not the flour
itself but the **starch behaviour**: starch-based blends turn pasty if underbaked and firm up
quickly once cool.

Neighbouring dishes to keep apart: **blondie** (no cocoa), **flourless chocolate cake**
(meringue or whipped-egg lift, much less dense), and the Nordic **kladdkaka** (a round-pan sticky
chocolate cake, cocoa-only, cakier crumb). Semper publishes a gluten-free kladdkaka, which is the
closest Nordic relative, not a brownie.

**Source weakness:** brownie technique is documented almost entirely in English food blogs of
mixed quality. The Norwegian and Swedish sources only cover flour: Møllerens, Semper, Toro and
supplier product sheets. Several search hits were content farms and were ignored. I could not
confirm what Meny or Sunkost stock today, only manufacturer distribution lists. Treat the matrix
as solid on technique and soft on availability.

| Flour amount | Small | ≈0.4 cup mixed flour/starch per 3 eggs (Noshtastic) to 150–180 g mix (Møllerens, Semper kladdkaka) | Fudgy versions lean on cocoa and chocolate for structure; the high end is cakier |
| Leavening | Little or none | None · ½–1 tsp baking powder · ½ tsp xanthan | King Arthur and Møllerens use 1 tsp baking powder; ATK uses xanthan; Leite's flourless uses baking soda |
| Chocolate source | Cocoa, melted chocolate, or both | Cocoa only (KA, Møllerens, Semper) · chocolate only (Leite's) · both (ATK, Wheat by the Wayside) | Chocolate-led versions are the ones sold as fudgy |
| Sugar | White granulated | White only · white + brown | White gives the shiniest crust; brown is moister but gives a less crisp top (Everything Just Baked, Eat with Clarity) |
| Shiny-top method | Dissolve the sugar, then beat with the eggs | Heat sugar into hot butter (KA, GF on a Shoestring) · beat eggs, then eggs + sugar, 1 min each (Joy Food Sunshine) · sugar melted into water (Eat with Clarity) | Same principle: dissolved sugar plus air gives a meringue-like crust. ## Base recipe

The synthesis before the first bake. One tray, 24 pieces of about 66 g each. Sized for a **22 × 32 cm
base** (≈700 cm², batter about 1.8 cm deep). Gram weights throughout; scale by base area ÷ 704.

**Chocolate base**
- 250 g unsalted butter
- 250 g dark chocolate, 70%, chopped
- 380 g white granulated sugar
- 100 g light brown sugar
- 6 g espresso beans, ground very fine (or 4 g instant espresso powder)
- 6.5 g fine salt (≈0.41% of total batter)

**Eggs**
- 250 g whole egg, beaten (about 5 eggs; weigh it)
- 20 g egg yolk (1 yolk)
- 10 g vanilla extract

**Dry**
- 60 g cocoa powder
- 110 g Møllerens Fin Melblanding glutenfri (see flour choice below)

**Chunks**
- 150 g dark chocolate, 70%, in 1 cm chunks

Total batter ≈ 1,590 g. Flour is 6.9% of it; fat (butter plus cocoa butter from the chocolate) is
about 25%.

**Scaling by pan base:** 20 × 30 cm ×0.85 (salt 5.5 g) · 22 × 32 ×1.00 · 23 × 33 ×1.08 (7.0 g) ·
25 × 35 ×1.24 (8.1 g) · 30 × 40 ×1.70 (11.1 g). Bake time shifts with depth, so check by signal.

**Method:** Heat the oven to 160 °C fan. Grease the tray and line with parchment, leaving
overhangs to lift the brownies out by. Melt the butter with the 250 g chocolate over low heat,
stir in both sugars, the ground espresso and the salt, and warm until the sugar is mostly
dissolved and the mixture is lukewarm, not hot. Whisk the eggs, yolk and vanilla in the stand mixer
for about 1 minute until pale, then stream in the warm chocolate mixture and beat on high for 3 to
4 minutes until thick, glossy and ribbon-like. Sift in the cocoa and flour and fold until no
streaks remain, then fold in the chunks. Rest the batter 15 minutes, spread into the tray and tap
it on the counter. Bake until the edges pull in slightly, the top has set to a matte crackled
film and the centre still wobbles a little (check from 28 minutes). Cool in the tray for 10
minutes, lift out on the parchment onto a rack, cool completely, then wrap airtight at room
temperature overnight. Cut with a hot knife. To serve, warm the pieces on a tray at 150 °C fan for
6 to 8 minutes.

**Flour choice, Norway.** A brownie needs little flour, so the blend matters less than in bread.
What matters is a predictable blend with nothing added that moves salt, sugar or texture.
- **Møllerens Fin Melblanding glutenfri — first choice.** Manufacturer states it is flour and
  starch only, with no added salt, sugar, fat or baking powder, no wheat starch, no milk, and is
  licensed by Norsk Cøliakiforening. Møllerens publishes its own gluten-free brownie recipe for
  it. Distribution lists name Rema, Joker, Kiwi, Meny, Spar and Bunnpris (2018 release) and the
  Fri-For shelf at Coop Prix, Mega, Marked, Obs and Extra (later release). **Not retrieved:** the
  full ingredient list and nut-trace statement. Check the pack.
- **Semper Fin Mix (Schär in Sweden) — fallback.** Cornstarch, rice flour, potato flour,
  modified potato starch, a little sugar, and guar, HPMC, alginate and pectin. The gums give
  binding without adding xanthan. The Swedish Schär listing says may contain soy. Semper's
  2021 brochure flags nut or almond traces on some products, and I could not tell which, so check
  the pack.
- **Avoid for this job:** Toro Fri Lys Melblanding. Per the supplier sheet it contains salt
  (0.83 g/100 g, so ≈0.9 g hidden in 110 g), rapeseed oil and FiberHUSK (psyllium), which gels.
  The hidden salt matters because salt has been off in past recipes, and the psyllium is my
  inference for why it would push toward gummy. Also skip every **Grov** blend (oats, sesame,
  fibre).

**Contested calls:**
- **No almond, no baking powder.** Almond is the dominant GF base in the sources but is off the
  brief. Leavening is dropped for density; the sources that use it are making a cakier brownie.
- **Chocolate and cocoa together.** The chocolate-led recipes are the fudgy ones, and 70% plus
  cocoa gives the deep bittersweet requested.
- **Extra yolk.** Adds fat and chew without extra water; the base recipes use whole eggs only,
  so this is my addition.
- **Sugar 380 g white / 100 g brown.** White for the shiny top, a little brown for moisture and
  chew. Sources say brown dulls the crust, so keep the brown share small.
- **Dry coffee, not liquid.** Estimated sugar to water is about 2.0:1 before any liquid coffee,
  right on the borderline of the one-source "≥2×" rule. A shot of espresso would push it below.
- **Bake to set, not raw.** Starches gelatinise well below the temperature a fudgy brownie reaches,
  so a truly raw centre is pasty, not gooey. The gooey should come from fat, sugar and chocolate
  that is soft when warm, which the reheat provides. This is my reasoning from general food
  science, not a sourced claim.
- **Instant-read target 88–90 °C at the centre.** My rule of thumb, unsourced. Log the reading in
  test 1 so it can be calibrated.
- **Cool out of the pan.** Barbone's rack trick; keeps carryover from tipping into gummy.
- **Salt.** 6.5 g fine salt is ≈0.41% of ≈1,590 g batter, assuming **unsalted butter** and a
  salt-free flour. If the butter is salted, subtract its salt (butter weight × label salt %).
  Add Maldon on top only to taste, after tasting.
- **Nut check.** Flour is one risk, but dark chocolate and cocoa often carry "may contain nuts"
  labels. Check the chocolate and cocoa packs, not just the flour.

## Open questions

1. **Does the shiny top survive overnight storage?** Sealed storage tends to soften it, and
   reheating won't restore the crust. Test room temperature airtight vs loosely covered.
2. **Flour dose.** 110 g (22 g per whole egg) is a deliberate low end. Too gooey or goopy at the
   centre → raise to 125–130 g; too firm → drop to 95 g.
3. **Bake endpoint.** Where does 88–90 °C at the centre actually land on the wobble and skewer
   signals, and how long does it take in the user's tray?
4. **Cold vs reheated.** Is cold chewier and better, or is the reheated gooey version clearly
   the target? Expect cold to be firmer after starch firming overnight.
5. **Møllerens Fin vs Semper Fin Mix.** Same result, or does the gum content change the chew?
6. **Coffee amount.** 6 g ground espresso is a guess for "enhance the chocolate" without tasting of
   coffee.
7. **Caramel option (if wanted).** A cooled caramel swirl added after spreading, about 120 g.
   Adds water and sugar, so risks a gummier centre. Check that any bought caramel or toffee has
   no nuts or almond traces.
8. **Meny and Sunkost stock.** Not verified. Check shelves for Møllerens Fin Melblanding first.
