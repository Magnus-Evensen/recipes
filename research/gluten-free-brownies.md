## Canonical definition

A dense, chocolate-led bar baked in a pan, sitting between cake and confection. The texture
spectrum is the whole argument: **fudgy** comes from a high ratio of fat and chocolate to flour,
**cakey** from more flour plus leavening, **chewy** from sugar and well-beaten eggs. The brief
here is fudgy + chewy + gooey, a shiny crackly top, bittersweet with coffee, chocolate chunks, no
nuts, gluten-free, **milk-free** (added 2026-11-06), baked the day before and reheated. The gooey
comes from fat and chocolate that is soft when warm, not from raw batter. See the contested calls
below.

Gluten is not load-bearing in a fudgy brownie. Flour is a small share of the batter and the
structure comes mostly from eggs and cocoa solids. One source goes further and calls this a recipe
where working without gluten is an advantage for chew (ckbk). The hard part is not the flour
itself but the **starch behaviour**: starch-based blends turn pasty if underbaked and firm up
quickly once cool.

Milk-free is cheap on the brownie side, because oil-based brownies are an established type and
King Arthur's own gluten-free mix says to swap the butter for vegetable oil to make it non-dairy.
The expensive part is **ingredient sourcing**: the default Norwegian baking chocolate contains
milk, and the obvious plant-butter blocks contain almond. See the chocolate gate and fat call below.

Neighbouring dishes to keep apart: **blondie** (no cocoa), **flourless chocolate cake**
(meringue or whipped-egg lift, much less dense), and the Nordic **kladdkaka** (a round-pan sticky
chocolate cake, cocoa-only, cakier crumb). Semper publishes a gluten-free kladdkaka, which is the
closest Nordic relative, not a brownie.

**Source weakness:** brownie technique is documented almost entirely in English food blogs of
mixed quality. The Norwegian and Swedish sources only cover flour: Møllerens, Semper, Toro and
supplier product sheets. Several search hits were content farms and were ignored. I could not
confirm what Meny or Sunkost stock today, only manufacturer distribution lists, and I could not
find a milk-free, nut-trace-free dark chocolate confirmed as sold in Norway. Treat the matrix as
solid on technique and soft on availability.

## Base recipe

The synthesis before the first bake. One tray, 24 pieces of about 64 g each. Sized for a **22 × 32 cm
base** (≈700 cm², batter about 1.8 cm deep). Gram weights throughout; scale by base area ÷ 704.

**Chocolate base**
- 205 g neutral oil (refined rapeseed or sunflower; not cold-pressed, which tastes strongly)
- 250 g dark chocolate ~70%, chopped, **no milk ingredients** (see the chocolate gate)
- 380 g white granulated sugar
- 100 g light brown sugar
- 6 g espresso beans, ground very fine (or 4 g instant espresso powder)
- 6.5 g fine salt (≈0.42% of total batter)

**Eggs**
- 250 g whole egg, beaten (about 5 eggs; weigh it)
- 20 g egg yolk (1 yolk)
- 10 g vanilla extract

**Dry**
- 60 g cocoa powder
- 110 g Møllerens Fin Melblanding glutenfri (see flour choice below)

**Chunks**
- 150 g of the same chocolate, in 1 cm chunks

Total batter ≈ 1,550 g. Flour is 7.1% of it; fat (oil plus cocoa butter from the chocolate) is
about 23%. The oil weighs 82% of the butter it replaces (250 g butter × 0.82).

**Scaling by pan base:** 20 × 30 cm ×0.85 (oil 175 g, salt 5.5 g) · 22 × 32 ×1.00 · 23 × 33 ×1.08
(oil 221 g, salt 7.0 g) · 25 × 35 ×1.24 (oil 255 g, salt 8.1 g) · 30 × 40 ×1.70 (oil 349 g, salt
11.1 g). Bake time shifts with depth, so check by signal.

**Method:** Heat the oven to 160 °C fan. Grease the tray and line with parchment, leaving
overhangs to lift the brownies out by. Melt the 250 g chocolate with the oil over low heat, stir in
the ground espresso, and let it cool to lukewarm. Whisk the eggs, yolk, vanilla, both sugars and
the salt in the stand mixer for 4 to 5 minutes until pale, thick and ribbon-like with no gritty
sugar left when rubbed between your fingers. Stream in the chocolate-oil on low and beat 1 minute
until glossy. Sift in the cocoa and flour and fold until no streaks remain, then fold in the
chunks. Rest the batter 15 minutes, spread into the tray and tap it on the counter. Bake until the
edges pull in slightly, the top has set to a matte crackled film and the centre still wobbles a
little (check from 28 minutes). Cool in the tray for 10 minutes, lift out on the parchment onto a
rack, cool completely, then wrap airtight at room temperature overnight. Cut with a hot knife.
To serve, warm the pieces on a tray at 150 °C fan for 6 to 8 minutes.

**Flour choice, Norway.** A brownie needs little flour, so the blend matters less than in bread.
What matters is a predictable blend with nothing added that moves salt, sugar or texture.
- **Møllerens Fin Melblanding glutenfri — first choice.** Manufacturer states it is flour and
  starch only, with no added salt, sugar, fat or baking powder, no wheat starch, no milk, and is
  licensed by Norsk Cøliakiforening. matoppskrift.no tags it vegan, which fits no milk ingredient
  but is not an explicit statement. Møllerens publishes its own gluten-free brownie recipe for
  it. Distribution lists name Rema, Joker, Kiwi, Meny, Spar and Bunnpris (2018 release) and the
  Fri-For shelf at Coop Prix, Mega, Marked, Obs and Extra (later release). **Not retrieved:** the
  full ingredient list and the "may contain" line. Check the pack for milk and nuts.
- **Semper Fin Mix (Schär in Sweden) — fallback.** Cornstarch, rice flour, potato flour,
  modified potato starch, a little sugar, and guar, HPMC, alginate and pectin. No milk ingredient
  in the list. The Swedish Schär listing says may contain soy. Semper's 2021 brochure flags nut or
  almond traces on some products, and I could not tell which, so check the pack.
- **Avoid for this job:** Toro Fri Lys Melblanding. Per the supplier sheet it contains salt
  (0.83 g/100 g, so ≈0.9 g hidden in 110 g), rapeseed oil and FiberHUSK (psyllium), which gels.
  The hidden salt matters because salt has been off in past recipes, and the psyllium is my
  inference for why it would push toward gummy. Also skip every **Grov** blend (oats, sesame,
  fibre).

**Chocolate gate (new).** The chocolate is now the main sourcing risk, because it has to be
gluten-safe, nut-safe and milk-free at once. What I found:
- **Freia mørk kokesjokolade 70% — out.** The Norwegian Foodstore listing shows milk fat and
  powdered milk in the ingredients, with "may contain gluten, nuts". The matoppskrift entry (2020)
  agrees: milk and soy, traces of gluten and nuts. This is the obvious Norwegian baking chocolate
  and it fails on three counts.
- **Lindt Excellence 70% — passes on ingredients only.** UK listing: cocoa mass, sugar, cocoa
  butter, vanilla; no milk ingredient, but "may contain traces of nuts, milk, soya and sesame".
  Fine if the restriction is about ingredients, not if traces matter.
- **So Free Perfectly Dark 72% — passes everything.** UK listing: cocoa mass, sugar, sunflower
  lecithin; labelled dairy-free, nut-free, gluten-free; may contain soya only. **Not verified as
  sold in Norway.** Pascha is a similar free-from brand; also unverified here.
- **Rule for any bar:** no melk, melkefett, melkepulver, fløte, laktose or myse in the
  ingredients, and read the "kan inneholde spor av" line for milk and nuts.
- **Fallback if nothing passes:** a cocoa-only brownie (no melted chocolate, no chunks), which
  needs only a clean cocoa powder. Not designed yet; it needs more oil and sugar to make up for
  the cocoa butter and sugar the chocolate was supplying.

**Contested calls:**
- **No almond, no baking powder.** Almond is the dominant GF base in the sources but is off the
  brief. Leavening is dropped for density; the sources that use it are making a cakier brownie.
- **Oil, not plant butter.** Naturli's vegan baking block lists almond (1%) and shea butter oil,
  with "may contain hazelnuts, cashew nuts and pistachio nuts" (UK listing). That is a nut risk on a nut-free brief, and
  I have not checked any plant butter sold in Norway. A single-ingredient oil has no hidden milk
  or nuts and is already in the kitchen. Cost: no butter flavour. In the AOL test, oil let sharper
  cocoa notes dominate, which suits bittersweet.
- **205 g oil, not 250.** Butter is ~80% fat and ~16% water; the AOL test found equal-volume
  oil swaps go greasy. The 0.82 factor is mine.
- **Sugar dissolved in the eggs, not the fat.** Oil has no water, so the heat-sugar-into-butter
  route no longer dissolves it. Whisking sugar and eggs longer follows the Joy Food Sunshine
  method. The shiny crust is not guaranteed; this is the main thing to watch in test 1. If it
  fails, gently warm the eggs and sugar over a pan of hot water to ~45 °C before whisking (my
  idea, untested).
- **Chocolate and cocoa together.** The chocolate-led recipes are the fudgy ones, and 70% plus
  cocoa gives the deep bittersweet requested.
- **Extra yolk.** Adds fat and chew without extra water; the base recipes use whole eggs only,
  so this is my addition.
- **Sugar 380 g white / 100 g brown.** White for the shiny top, a little brown for moisture and
  chew. Sources say brown dulls the crust, so keep the brown share small.
- **Dry coffee, not liquid.** Estimated sugar to water is about 2.4:1 with oil (water from the
  eggs only, ≈197 g against 480 g sugar), above the one-source "≥2×" rule. A shot of espresso
  would eat into that margin.
- **Bake to set, not raw.** Starches gelatinise well below the temperature a fudgy brownie reaches,
  so a truly raw centre is pasty, not gooey. The gooey should come from fat, sugar and chocolate
  that is soft when warm, which the reheat provides. This is my reasoning from general food
  science, not a sourced claim.
- **Instant-read target 88–90 °C at the centre.** My rule of thumb, unsourced. Log the reading in
  test 1 so it can be calibrated.
- **Cool out of the pan.** Barbone's rack trick; keeps carryover from tipping into gummy.
- **Salt.** 6.5 g fine salt is ≈0.42% of ≈1,550 g batter. Oil is unsalted, so the earlier
  salted-butter caveat is gone; the flour is salt-free per the manufacturer. Add Maldon on top only
  to taste, after tasting.
- **Nut check.** Dark chocolate and cocoa often carry "may contain nuts" labels. Check the
  chocolate and cocoa packs, not just the flour.

## Open questions

1. **How strict is the milk-free and nut-free brief?** Ingredient-level (no milk or nut ingredients,
   traces tolerated) or allergy-level (traces matter)? It decides whether Lindt-type chocolate is
   acceptable or a free-from bar or the cocoa-only version is needed.
2. **Does the shiny top survive oil, and overnight storage?** Oil removes the hot-butter step, and
   sealed storage tends to soften the crust. Test the 4–5 minute whisk first, then warming the eggs;
   test room temperature airtight vs loosely covered.
3. **Flour dose.** 110 g (22 g per whole egg) is a deliberate low end. Too gooey or goopy at the
   centre → raise to 125–130 g; too firm → drop to 95 g.
4. **Water add-back.** Oil brings none of butter's ≈40 g water. If the batter looks stiff or the
   crumb dry, try 30–40 g water; otherwise leave it out.
5. **Bake endpoint.** Where does 88–90 °C at the centre actually land on the wobble and skewer
   signals, and how long does it take in the user's tray?
6. **Cold vs reheated.** Is cold chewier and better, or is the reheated gooey version clearly
   the target? Expect cold to be firmer after starch firming overnight, and oil sets firmer when
   cold than when warm.
7. **Møllerens Fin vs Semper Fin Mix.** Same result, or does the gum content change the chew?
8. **Coffee amount.** 6 g ground espresso is a guess for "enhance the chocolate" without tasting of
   coffee.
9. **Coconut oil as the fat.** A common oil-brownie choice (The Salted Sweets); not tested here and
   a possible concern if "no nuts" is read broadly. Coconut oil is solid at room temperature, so the
   brownies would be firmer when cold.
10. **Caramel option (if wanted).** A cooled caramel swirl added after spreading, about 120 g. Most
    caramel contains butter or cream, so it must be made milk-free, which adds work. Adds water and
    sugar, so risks a gummier centre.
11. **Meny and Sunkost stock.** Not verified. Check shelves for Møllerens Fin Melblanding first,
    and for a qualifying chocolate.
