---
title: Pork chili con carne
date: 2026-11-01
cuisine: north-america
foodType: Stew
season: Winter
tags:

servings: 4
prepTimeMinutes: 30
cookTimeMinutes: 150
ingredients: |-
  Chili:
  - 1.1kg Pork shoulder (svinenakke), cut into 2cm cubes, fat left on
  - 8g Salt
  - 2 tablespoons Neutral oil or lard
  - 100g Bacon, diced (optional)
  - 1 Large onion, finely chopped
  - 5 Garlic cloves, minced
  - 1 tablespoon Cumin seeds
  - 1 teaspoon Mexican oregano
  - 1 Bay leaf
  - 1 tablespoon Fish sauce
  - 1 can (400g) Crushed tomatoes
  - 1 can (400g) Black or pinto beans, drained
  - 1 teaspoon Cider vinegar (optional)
  - Salt to finish

  Chile base:
  - 4 Dried ancho chiles
  - 4 Dried guajillo chiles
  - 2 Dried pasilla chiles
  - 500 ml Low-salt chicken stock (never beef)
---
1. Toss the pork with the salt and leave it while you do everything else

2. Stem and seed the chiles and tear them flat. Toast them in a dry pan until fragrant and pliable (throw out any that blacken, they turn bitter)

3. Toast the cumin seeds in the same pan until fragrant and grind them

4. Pour the hot stock over the chiles and leave until completely soft, about 25 minutes

5. Blend the chiles with all the stock until smooth

6. Fry the bacon in a heavy pot until crisp, then take it out and keep the fat (can be skipped, but you lose some smoke and depth)

7. Pat the pork dry and brown it hard in batches. Don't crowd the pot or it steams instead of browning

8. Lower the heat and cook the onion in the fat until soft

9. Add the garlic, cumin and oregano and stir for 1 minute

10. Add the chile purée and stir constantly until it darkens and thickens

11. Return the pork and bacon with the tomatoes, bay leaf and fish sauce. Bring to a bare simmer with the lid slightly ajar

12. Simmer until a cube breaks apart when you press it with a spoon. Usually 2-2.5 hours, longer if your shoulder is lean. Firm is not done

13. Press a third to half of the cubes against the side of the pot. This thickens the sauce, no masa needed

14. Add the beans for the last 20 minutes

15. Take out the bay leaf, taste, then add the vinegar if it tastes flat and salt to finish

Short on guajillo or pasilla? Make up the weight with more ancho, fry 1½ tablespoons sweet paprika with the purée, and stir about 10g dark chocolate (70%) into the pot at the end. I've made it this way and it was great.

Traditionally, Texas chili is eaten pretty bare, with chopped onion, cheese and crackers, and northern Mexican chile colorado comes with rice and tortillas. I serve it with tortilla chips, guacamole and diced raw onion, which is Tex-Mex rather than traditional, but works really well. If you're used to the European style, serve it with rice, otherwise it gets quite meat-heavy.

Tastes better the next day and freezes well. Lift the fat off the top once it's cold.
