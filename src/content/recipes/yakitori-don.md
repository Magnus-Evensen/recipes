---
title: Yakitori don (chicken and leek rice bowl)
date: 2026-11-09
cuisine: japanese
foodType: Rice bowl
tags:
  - Quick
  - Comfort
servings: 3
prepTimeMinutes: 15
cookTimeMinutes: 20
ingredients: |-
  Tare:
  - 45ml Light soy sauce (preferably Japanese)
  - 45ml Mirin
  - 45ml Ryorishu (sake: a pinch more salt; mirin: less sugar, more salt to taste)
  - 12g Sugar
  - 10g Ginger, finely grated

  Chicken and leek:
  - 400g Boneless, skinless chicken thigh, cut into 3cm pieces
  - 1 pinch Fine salt
  - 5g Corn or potato starch
  - 250g Leek (2 thin ones), white and pale green part, cut into 2.5cm rounds
  - 1 tablespoon Neutral oil
  - 50ml Water

  To serve:
  - 270g Japanese short-grain rice, uncooked (basmati works too)
  - 3 Egg yolks
  - 1 Nori sheet, cut into thin strips
  - Shichimi togarashi
  - A bit of the inner leek, very finely sliced and rinsed (optional)
---
1. Salt the chicken and leave it while the rice cooks

2. Mix the soy, mirin, ryorishu, sugar and ginger until the sugar dissolves

3. Heat a large frying pan over medium-high heat with half the oil and fry the leek rounds cut-side down until golden on both faces

4. Add the water, put a lid on and steam until a knife goes through the leek with no resistance (add a splash more water if it dries out). Take the leek out and wipe the pan

5. Pat the chicken dry and toss it in the starch

6. Heat the rest of the oil and add the chicken in one layer. Leave it alone until it lets go of the pan, then flip and cook until browned on both sides and just about cooked through

7. Pour in the tare, put the leek back and turn everything through it for a minute or two, until it is a thin syrup that still pools when you tilt the pan. Take it off the heat earlier than feels right, the starch keeps thickening it (and don't add water at this point, that's how it goes gluey)

8. Fill the bowls with rice, add the chicken and leek and spoon the sauce over so some of it soaks into the rice

9. Top with an egg yolk, shichimi and the sliced leek if using, and finish with the nori on top

Traditionally this is skin-on thigh grilled over charcoal, with Japanese long onion instead of leek. Skinless thigh in a pan ends up closer to teriyaki, but the starch and a hard sear keep it on the right side. The leek needs steaming on its own first, otherwise it's still raw by the time the sauce is done.

This does the two of us for dinner with nothing left over. Go up to 600g chicken if you want lunch the next day. It keeps fine in the fridge, but I wouldn't freeze it.
