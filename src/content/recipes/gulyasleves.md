---
title: Gulyásleves (Hungarian goulash soup)
date: 2026-11-05
cuisine: hungarian
foodType: Soup
season: Autumn
tags:
  - Braised
  - Freezer-friendly
  - Meal-prep
servings: 4
prepTimeMinutes: 30
cookTimeMinutes: 150
ingredients: |-
  Base:
  - 70g Smoked streaky bacon, diced
  - 2 Medium onions, finely diced
  - 1 Sweet pointed pepper, diced
  - 1 Tomato, diced
  - 3 Garlic cloves, crushed
  - 13g Sweet Hungarian paprika
  - 3g Caraway seeds, ground
  - 1 Hot green chilli, sliced
  - Neutral oil (only if the bacon runs dry)

  Meat:
  - 950g Pork shoulder, trimmed and cut into 3cm cubes
  - 6g Fine salt

  Soup:
  - 2 Carrots, in 1cm dice
  - 70g Celeriac, in 1cm dice (skip it and you lose the sweet, faintly bitter note parsley root would have given)
  - 400g Potato, Folva or Sava, in 2cm dice
  - Roughly 1.3 l Water
  - 2g Fine salt, to finish
---
1. Render the bacon in a Dutch oven over medium heat until it gives up its fat and the pieces start to crisp

2. Add the onions and cook over medium-low until they collapse completely and turn golden — they should end up soft enough to disappear into the broth (this is where the body comes from, so give it the full half hour even when it looks done early)

3. Add the pepper and tomato and cook until the tomato darkens and loses its raw edge, then stir in the garlic for a minute

4. Pull the pot off the heat, stir in the paprika, caraway and chilli, and get a ladle of water in within a few seconds (burnt paprika turns the whole pot bitter and there is no saving it)

5. Back on the heat. Add the pork and the salt and stir until the surfaces turn pale — don't brown it, the browning in this dish happens to the base rather than the meat

6. Barely cover with water and simmer until it has almost gone and the fat separates out red at the edges, then add more and repeat

7. Start checking at 75 minutes — it's ready when a cube gives completely under a spoon (pork has a window that beef doesn't, and past it it goes stringy rather than better)

8. Add the carrot and celeriac, then the potato five minutes behind them

9. Top up with hot water to soup length, keeping it shorter than looks right, and simmer until the potato is just tender (overcooked potato clouds the broth)

10. Taste and finish with the rest of the salt

Serve with bread. If it feels thin, crush four or five potato cubes against the side of the pot — that releases enough starch to thicken it without losing the dice.

Better the next day, and leaving it overnight also lets the pork fat set on top so you can lift it off in one piece. Freeze the base before the potato goes in, in 700-800ml batches — potato goes mealy after a thaw, so add it fresh when you reheat.
